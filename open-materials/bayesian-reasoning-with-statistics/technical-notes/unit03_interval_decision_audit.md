# Unit 3 interval and decision audit

The unchanged Unit 2 CSV gives `n=12` and `y=8` binary reported contacts. The common θ target is a probability under a comparable-regime Bernoulli sampling model; neither route identifies an orientation effect or a campus rate. The declared Beta(2,2) prior and binomial likelihood yield Beta(10,6) posterior. The central equal-tail posterior interval has endpoints `qbeta(.025,10,6)` and `qbeta(.975,10,6)`, respectively `0.38380373254115413` and `0.8366356761404865`. These numbers specify a conditional distribution, not a coverage procedure.

The familiar route uses a Wilson score interval rather than a Wald interval. Let `p=y/n`, `z=Phi^{-1}(.975)`, `D=1+z²/n`; then center `(p+z²/(2n))/D` and half-width `z sqrt(p(1-p)/n+z²/(4n²))/D`. Endpoints are `0.39062208887279953` and `0.8618799089087867`. Nominal 95% coverage is a repeated-sampling property under the binomial model; discrete-data coverage depends on θ and n. No posterior probability is attached to this realized Wilson interval.

The illustrative magnitude is θ>0.60, with exact primary posterior probability `0.5967844495851521`. For integer beta shapes, `F(x;a,b)=sum_{j=a}^{a+b-1} choose(a+b-1,j)x^j(1-x)^(a+b-1-j)`. The independent Python audit uses this finite sum with rational `x=3/5` for exact threshold mass and binary search for 0.025/0.975 quantiles; it uses standard-library NormalDist for the Wilson z. The primary Python route instead uses SciPy beta and normal functions; base R uses its beta and normal functions and an independent audit of raw rows.

Under stipulated toy payoffs +1 when θ>0.60 and −1 otherwise, with no action worth zero, expected utility is `2 Pr(θ>0.60)-1`. The primary value is `0.19356889917030418`. Beta(1,1) updates to Beta(9,5), yielding mass `0.646958153728` and value `0.29391630745600006`. Beta(8,8) updates to Beta(16,12), yielding mass `0.38726771570698787` and value `-0.2254645685860242`. Those priors are pedagogical alternatives, not elicited external evidence. A real action would need measured benefits, harms, costs, a relevant population and an effect or outcome target that matches the intervention question.

Unit 2's prior predictive `Pr(Y>=10 of 12)=2/13` and fitted posterior predictive `Pr(Yrep>=8 of 12)=0.5192982456` remain checks of this simplified model's implications, with no held-out evaluation. They do not establish conditional independence, accurate self-report, population coverage or causal identification. Every Unit 3 calculation is deterministic/exact and has no MCMC chains or MCSE. The model spec and full-precision anchor are in `model-specs/unit03_intervals_decision.json` and `expected-results/unit03_intervals_decision.json`.
