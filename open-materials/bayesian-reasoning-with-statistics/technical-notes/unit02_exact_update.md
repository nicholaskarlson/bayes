# Unit 2 exact update and audit note

The reader case has `n=12`, `y=8`, with one binary report per fictional volunteer. The likelihood for the count is `choose(n,y) theta^y (1-theta)^(n-y)`. Under `theta ~ Beta(a,b)`, the posterior density is proportional to `theta^(a+y-1) (1-theta)^(b+n-y-1)`. The primary `(a,b)=(2,2)` gives `Beta(10,6)`. The beta-binomial predictive mass for a new count `k` out of `m` is `choose(m,k) B(a_post+k,b_post+m-k)/B(a_post,b_post)`, with `B` the beta function. Substitute the prior shapes for prior predictive mass.

The 95% interval uses 0.025 and 0.975 beta quantiles. The primary posterior has mean `10/16`, `Pr(theta>1/2)=0.84912109375`, interval `[0.3838037325,0.8366356761]`, and new-twelve `Pr(Yrep>=8)=0.5192982456`. Before the data, `Pr(Y>=10)=2/13` and `Pr(Y<=2 or Y>=10)=4/13`. For integer beta shapes, the independent audit evaluates the beta CDF as a finite binomial sum, then bisects it for quantiles; its predictive masses use exact fractions. The primary analyzer instead uses SciPy beta and beta-binomial functions, and base R uses its beta functions. No random draws, convergence diagnostics or MCSE enter these checks.

The two sensitivity priors Beta(1,1) and Beta(8,8) update to Beta(9,5) and Beta(16,12). The implied posterior probabilities above a half are 0.8665771484 and 0.7789658308, respectively. These priors are teaching contrasts, not elicited real-world knowledge. An after-the-fact prior selection or cutoff cannot justify action. The posterior predictive comparison uses the same observed count as the fit; its apparent compatibility is a weak in-sample check. Conditional independence, equal propensity, report validity and a population match remain unverified.
